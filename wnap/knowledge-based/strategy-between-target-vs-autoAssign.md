# YNAB Strategy: Assigned vs. Targets

This is an excellent question! Many new YNAB users wonder: *"Why bother setting a Target when I can just type the amount directly into the Assigned column?"*

In reality, **Assigned** is the action, while **Target** is the plan. Think of it like this:

## 1. "Assigned" is the Water; "Target" is the Line on the Glass
* **Assigned:** The actual money you just took from your wallet (Ready to Assign) and put into a pocket (Category). If you type $300, that pocket now physically holds $300.
* **Target:** The pre-drawn line on that pocket. It’s a reminder saying: *"Hey, this pocket should eventually hold $300."*

## 2. The "Power" of Targets vs. Manual Entry

### A. Visual Reminders (Visual Cues)
* **Without a Target:** If you haven't added money yet (Assigned = 0), the Available column stays **Gray**. It looks "normal," making it very easy to forget that you haven't planned for your electricity bill yet.
* **With a Target:** If you haven't assigned enough money, the Available column turns **Yellow**. It acts like a warning light: *"You haven't finished this goal yet!"* It only turns **Green** once the requirement is fully met.

### B. The Power of the "Underfunded" Button (Automation)
This is the biggest benefit. Imagine you have 30 categories:
* **Without Targets:** Every single month, you have to remember: *"How much was the electric bill? How much is rent?"* and manually type 30 different numbers.
* **With Targets:** You simply click the **Underfunded** card and hit **Auto-Assign**. YNAB fills in the exact amounts for all 30 categories in one second. You no longer need to memorize your bills.

### C. Automatic Math for Large Expenses (Rule 2: Embrace True Expenses)
Suppose you have a $1,200 insurance bill due in December (6 months away).
* **Manual Entry:** You have to do the math yourself: $1,200 / 6 months = $200 per month.
* **Using a Target:** You just tell YNAB, *"I need $1,200 by December."* YNAB calculates the $200 for you. If you are short on cash this month and only assign $100, next month YNAB **automatically** recalculates that you now need $220 to stay on track. You don't have to lift a finger.

---

## When SHOULD (and shouldn't) you set a Target?

| Category Type | Use a Target? | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fixed Bills** (Rent, Netflix...) | **YES** | To use Auto-Assign for speed. |
| **Periodic Expenses** (Insurance, Car Repairs) | **STRONGLY YES** | To let YNAB break down the monthly savings automatically. |
| **Variable Spending** (Dining out, Shopping) | **OPTIONAL** | To set a "ceiling" (e.g., don't spend over $200/month). |
| **One-time unexpected costs** | **NO** | Just move money directly into "Assigned" and move on. |

---

**Summary:** When you manually enter money into **Assigned**, you are performing Rule 1 (*Give Every Dollar a Job*). When you set a **Target**, you are building a blueprint so that YNAB can run on autopilot every month without relying on your memory.
